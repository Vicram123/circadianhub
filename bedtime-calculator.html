How the bedtime is worked out

The calculator takes your wake-up time, subtracts the number of cycles multiplied by 90 minutes, then subtracts your fall-asleep time. Five or six cycles (7.5 to 9 hours) are marked as recommended because most adults need seven to nine hours.

What can shift your ideal bedtime

Age, recent sleep debt, caffeine timing and your natural chronotype all change how much sleep you need. If you feel unrefreshed after a week of using a suggested time, move your bedtime 15 to 30 minutes earlier or later and see how you feel.

Frequently asked questions

Is 6 hours of sleep enough?

Most adults do best with seven to nine hours. Six hours (four cycles) is the shortest option shown, and is better treated as an occasional compromise than a routine.

Does the 90-minute rule really work?

Cycle length averages about 90 minutes but varies between people and from night to night, so treat the times as a good starting estimate rather than an exact rule.

What if I can't fall asleep in 14 minutes?

Tick the box under the calculator to enter a longer fall-asleep time, and the bedtimes move earlier to match.
